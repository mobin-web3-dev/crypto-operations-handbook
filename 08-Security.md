# Security in Crypto Operations

## Introduction

Security is one of the most important responsibilities in crypto operations.

Digital assets are irreversible once transferred on the blockchain. Because of this, preventing security incidents is far more effective than trying to recover funds after an attack.

Operations teams work closely with security, compliance, and customer support teams to protect user accounts and company assets.

---

## Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-Factor Authentication adds an extra layer of protection beyond a password.

Common methods include:

* Authenticator Apps
* Hardware Security Keys
* Email Verification
* SMS Verification (less secure)

Users should enable 2FA whenever possible.

---

## Phishing Attacks

Phishing attempts trick users into revealing sensitive information.

Examples include:

* Fake exchange websites
* Fake login pages
* Fake customer support agents
* Malicious emails

Always verify official website URLs before entering credentials.

---

## Social Engineering

Attackers sometimes manipulate users instead of attacking technology.

Examples:

* Pretending to be customer support
* Requesting verification codes
* Asking for recovery phrases
* Creating fake urgency

Operations teams should never ask users for private keys or recovery phrases.

---

## Private Keys and Recovery Phrases

Private keys and recovery phrases provide complete access to a wallet.

Best practices:

* Never share them.
* Never store them in cloud storage.
* Never send them through chat or email.

Anyone with access to these credentials controls the wallet.

---

## Address Verification

Before confirming any withdrawal:

* Verify the destination address.
* Double-check the blockchain network.
* Compare the first and last characters of the address.
* Avoid copying addresses from untrusted sources.

Small verification mistakes can result in permanent loss of funds.

---

## Address Poisoning

Address poisoning is a scam where attackers send small transactions from addresses that closely resemble legitimate wallet addresses.

Users may accidentally copy the attacker's address from their transaction history.

Always verify the complete destination address before sending funds.

---

## Clipboard Malware

Some malware monitors the clipboard and automatically replaces copied wallet addresses with an attacker's address.

Users should always compare the pasted address with the intended destination before confirming a transaction.

---

## Device Security

Good device security includes:

* Keeping software updated
* Using antivirus software
* Locking devices with strong passwords
* Avoiding unknown applications
* Installing software only from trusted sources

---

## Withdrawal Whitelists

Some platforms allow users to create a whitelist of approved withdrawal addresses.

When enabled, withdrawals can only be sent to pre-approved wallet addresses.

This significantly reduces the risk of unauthorized transfers.

---

## Security Best Practices

* Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
* Use strong, unique passwords.
* Keep recovery phrases offline.
* Verify wallet addresses carefully.
* Use official applications only.
* Be cautious of phishing attempts.
* Monitor account activity regularly.

---

## Real-world Operations Notes

Many security incidents are not caused by technical vulnerabilities but by human error.

Common support cases include phishing attacks, clipboard malware, fake customer support impersonation, and users exposing recovery phrases.

Operations teams help users recognize these risks, investigate suspicious activity, and apply security procedures to protect accounts.
