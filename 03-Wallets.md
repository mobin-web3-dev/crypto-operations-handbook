# Crypto Wallets

## What is a Crypto Wallet?

A crypto wallet is a tool that allows users or organizations to store, send, and receive digital assets.

A wallet does not actually store cryptocurrency. Instead, it stores the cryptographic keys that provide access to assets recorded on the blockchain.

---

## Hot Wallet

A hot wallet is connected to the internet.

It is commonly used for:

* Daily deposits
* Withdrawals
* Fast transaction processing

Advantages:

* Fast access
* Convenient for daily operations

Disadvantages:

* Higher security risk
* Internet exposure

---

## Cold Wallet

A cold wallet remains offline and is mainly used for long-term asset storage.

Advantages:

* Very high security
* Protection against online attacks

Disadvantages:

* Slower access
* Manual transfer process

---

## Custodial Wallet

A custodial wallet is managed by a third party, such as a cryptocurrency exchange.

The service provider controls the private keys.

Examples include wallets provided by centralized exchanges.

---

## Non-Custodial Wallet

In a non-custodial wallet, users control their own private keys.

This gives users full ownership but also full responsibility.

If the recovery phrase is lost, access to the assets may be permanently lost.

---

## Public Address

A public address is used to receive cryptocurrency.

It can be safely shared with others.

---

## Private Key

A private key provides full access to the wallet.

It must always remain secret.

Anyone with the private key can control the wallet.

---

## Recovery Phrase (Seed Phrase)

A recovery phrase is a series of words used to restore access to a wallet.

It should never be shared or stored online.

---

## Wallet Security Best Practices

* Never share private keys.
* Never share the recovery phrase.
* Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) whenever available.
* Verify wallet addresses before sending funds.
* Be cautious of phishing websites and fake wallet applications.
* Keep software up to date.

---

## Why Wallet Knowledge Matters

Crypto Operations teams regularly interact with wallet infrastructure.

Understanding different wallet types helps operators:

* Investigate deposits
* Process withdrawals
* Identify wallet-related issues
* Support customers more effectively
