# Common User Mistakes in Crypto

## Introduction

Many support requests are caused by simple user mistakes rather than technical problems.

Understanding these common mistakes helps Operations and Customer Support teams resolve issues faster while educating users on safe cryptocurrency practices.

---

## Sending Funds on the Wrong Network

One of the most common mistakes is selecting an unsupported blockchain network.

Example:

A user sends USDT using BEP20 to a platform that only supports ERC20.

Possible Result:

* Deposit may not be credited.
* Manual recovery may be required.
* Recovery is not always possible.

---

## Forgetting Memo or Destination Tag

Some cryptocurrencies require an additional identifier.

Examples:

* XRP Destination Tag
* XLM Memo

If the Memo or Tag is missing, the platform cannot automatically assign the deposit to the correct account.

---

## Sending Unsupported Assets

Users sometimes send cryptocurrencies or tokens that are not supported by the receiving platform.

Possible Result:

* Funds may not appear in the account.
* Recovery may require manual intervention.
* Recovery may not be possible.

---

## Copying the Wrong Wallet Address

Users may accidentally:

* Copy the wrong address.
* Paste an outdated address.
* Fall victim to clipboard malware.

Always verify both the beginning and end of the wallet address before sending funds.

---

## Ignoring Confirmation Requirements

Some users expect deposits to appear immediately.

However, most platforms require blockchain confirmations before crediting deposits.

Waiting for confirmations is a normal part of blockchain processing.

---

## Falling for Phishing Scams

Attackers often create fake websites or impersonate support representatives.

Common warning signs include:

* Requests for passwords
* Requests for recovery phrases
* Suspicious website addresses
* Urgent messages demanding immediate action

---

## Sharing Sensitive Information

Users should never share:

* Private Keys
* Recovery Phrases
* Two-Factor Authentication Codes
* Passwords

Legitimate support teams never request this information.

---

## Sending Large Transfers Without Testing

For large transfers, sending a small test transaction first helps verify:

* Wallet address
* Blockchain network
* Platform compatibility

This simple step can prevent costly mistakes.

---

## Not Saving the Transaction Hash (TXID)

The Transaction Hash is essential for investigating deposits and withdrawals.

Without it, troubleshooting becomes significantly more difficult.

Users should always keep the TXID until the transaction is completed successfully.

---

## Real-world Operations Notes

A large percentage of customer support tickets involve user mistakes rather than platform failures.

By identifying common patterns—such as incorrect networks, missing Memos, unsupported assets, or phishing attempts—Operations teams can resolve issues more efficiently while helping users avoid repeating the same mistakes.
