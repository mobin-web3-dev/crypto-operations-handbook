# Blockchain Basics

## What is a Blockchain?

A blockchain is a distributed digital ledger that records transactions across a network of computers.

Each transaction is grouped into a block. Once a block is verified and added to the chain, its data cannot be changed without changing every following block, making blockchain highly secure and transparent.

---

## Why is Blockchain Important in Crypto Operations?

Crypto Operations teams work with blockchain transactions every day.

Understanding how blockchains work helps operators:

* Verify deposits and withdrawals
* Check transaction confirmations
* Identify network delays
* Investigate failed or pending transactions
* Support users with transaction-related issues

---

## Key Concepts

### Block

A block is a collection of confirmed transactions that has been added to the blockchain.

---

### Transaction Hash (TXID)

Every blockchain transaction has a unique identifier called a Transaction Hash or TXID.

Operations teams often use the TXID to track and verify transactions.

---

### Confirmation

A transaction becomes more secure as additional blocks are added after it.

Most exchanges require a specific number of confirmations before crediting a deposit.

---

### Network Fee

Every blockchain transaction requires a network fee paid to validators or miners.

The fee depends on the blockchain and current network activity.

---

## Public Block Explorers

A block explorer allows anyone to view blockchain transactions.

Examples include:

* Tronscan (TRON)
* Etherscan (Ethereum)
* BscScan (BNB Smart Chain)

Operations teams frequently use block explorers to investigate user transactions.

---

## Summary

A basic understanding of blockchain technology is essential for anyone working in Crypto Operations.

It helps teams troubleshoot issues, verify transactions, and provide accurate support to users.
